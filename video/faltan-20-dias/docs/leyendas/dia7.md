# Día 7 · El Nahual · La sombra no miente

> Like every night, don Fermín walks past the tiendita. The half-lowered shutter cuts off his head, so we never see his face, but the wall across the street shows his shadow. When the village goes silent, he stands still and his shadow turns to look at us. He turns three times, his sarape falls empty, and a coyote walks out of it. Then its shadow comes back, upright, growing, toward the light, toward us. When we dare to raise the shutter, his sombrero and cane hang from the hook of our own door, and their giant shadow settles into a 7.

**Duración:** 45.5 s + outro

## Recurso

ONE UNCUT SHOT, crouched behind a half-lowered tiendita shutter, inside a single-light shadow world that stays physically consistent. (1) The shutter's lower edge always crops the living at the chest, while the bulb, hung above head height, puts their head shadows on the wall just below that edge. The real man and his true shadow share the frame, and we can never check his face. (2) The shadow has its own rig. It mirrors him 1:1 until the moment it detaches, which is the first big scare. (3) Shadow size tells distance from the light, and the film teaches this as a rule («Si su sombra crece… es que viene por ti»). The rule pays off twice: when the upright creature closes in, and at the end, when the gigantic shadow of his hat and cane tells us they are hanging right at our own door. (4) The shutter is the edit. Three stuck pulls narrow the frame as fear rises, the slam is the only black, and raising it is the reveal. In the black, its perforated band still glows with the swinging bulb and shows an eye looking in. (5) Sound duality: a human rhythm (huarache, huarache, cane tap) and a second rhythm of padded paws with claw ticks. The paws hide under his steps, grow, play alone once he is a coyote, and fuse into two-legged steps with claws during the approach. (6) The witness ('you') is never shown, only felt: handheld breathing that freezes in the held breath, leaning to follow the coyote, the hands jerking the shutter down. (7) The number is the shadow of what the nahual left behind, written by the bulb's dying pendulum.

## Dirección de arte

«Grabado nocturno de pueblo» (village night engraving): single-source chiaroscuro anchored on the brand palette. Light: warm tungsten from the unseen bulb (core #F5C242 rolling to #F26D3D, inverse-square falloff from the upper left) against a teal moonlit ambient (#1F5C48 darkened, a cool #16303A in deep shadow). Shadows are never pure black. Their floor is deep brown #231305, lifted by the teal fill. Cast shadows multiply as #231305 at 0.88 with physical penumbra. A Posada-style engraving hatch lives only in the shadows (Post hatch 0.22, hatchScale 5, hatchAngle −0.6, hatchInk #231305), so the darkness looks printed and the lights stay clean. Wall: whitewashed cal, lit #E0BB8F/#D9CDB6, peeling in noise-shaped patches that show terracotta adobe bricks (#A83A25 with darker mortar). It has hairline ridged-noise cracks, vertical water stains, a terracotta rodapié band along its base, and a rusted iron grille over closed green wooden shutters at the right (x 760–1000, y 520–900) that casts its own small sharp shadow. Street: river-stone empedrado with warm specular glints near the light, fading to teal, with cempasúchil petals scattered toward a far door. Far left: the callejón vanishes into layered teal fog, with a distant lamp, a power line and a string of papel picado (flores y rombos, bone and orange) silhouetted and swaying. Shutter: corrugated steel painted faded tiendita green #1F5C48. Ribs are 38 px with per-rib gradients, rust streaks and drips in #A83A25, and a perforated band (6 px holes on a 14 px grid). Its worn bottom lip and handle catch a hot rim of bulb light at the top-left of the gap, the only place the light itself is felt, with halation leaking in. Foreground: buckets of cempasúchil for sale on the sidewalk, defocused (28 px) at the bottom of frame. Characters are painted 2D silhouettes using paintShape: body gradient, inner shadow away from the light, an orange rim on the bulb side and teal on the far side. About 180 contour fur strokes on the coyote and dog, hand-drawn fringe on the sarape. Lens: shallow DOF, bloom on the lip spill and on eyes only, halation 0.25, grain 0.07, vignette 0.4, 1 px chromatic aberration at the edges (6 px spike at the scare). Grade: lifted warm mids, teal shadows, gold highlights: a Mexican Caravaggio in a callejón. No Halloween iconography: coyote, dog, tecolote, cempasúchil, papel picado and candles only. Type: Rye in gold #F5C242 with an orange glow for the title and lockup; Alike in bone #D9CDB6 for narrative lines, accent words in #F26D3D. There are three text bands, all inside the safe zone. The shutter band (block tops 276–360, ending ≤ 430) sits over dark metal. The bottom band (block tops 1400–1479, ending ≤ 1558) sits over the near cobblestones. The lockup splits around the wall: «FALTAN» on the shutter, the 7 on the wall, «DÍAS» and the date over the street.

## Personajes

- **name:** Don Fermín (the nahual, human form) · **how_shown:** Partial views only. Mid-street, from the chest down: the shutter line always crops his head. In the hook he first exists only as a gigantic shadow, then as a huge defocused shape (sarape fringe, a wrinkled brown hand on a gnarled mezquite cane) entering from the left edge. He is an old campesino: white manta trousers rolled at the ankle, worn leather huaraches, a striped wool sarape (terracotta, gold, green and bone bands with fringe) over the shoulders. His sombrero is never seen on him; it enters frame only when it falls. Never a face. · **motion:** Natural old-man gait on a 1.15 s cycle. The left leg limps (58/42 stance split), the cane plants with each left foot strike, and he leans forward 6°. Legs are ik2 with foot-lock and heel-toe roll (no sliding). The pelvis bobs 5 px at twice the step frequency, with 3 px lateral sway. The sarape is simCloth pinned at the shoulders, lagging about 0.15 s, plus 14 simChain fringe strands. To stop, he decelerates over two steps, the pelvis dips 4 px and recovers over 10 frames, then he re-grips the cane once. During the decoupling he is utterly still and does not breathe; only the fringe moves in gust(). Transformation: three spins on the spot, accelerating (1.5 s, 1.3 s, 1.2 s). The feet pivot heel to toe and the visible leg width follows |cos(spin)|. The sarape flares into a spinning skirt whose radius grows with ω². On the third spin it drops like a curtain and collapses empty.
- **name:** Su sombra (the shadow that doesn't lie) · **how_shown:** His shadow projected from the bulb onto the adobe wall: about 1.1x his screen size, about 220 px to his right, head just below the shutter edge, the sombrero crown cut by it. His leg shadows lie on the street and bend up onto the wall base. From 7.2 s a thin bushy tail sways under the shadow's sarape hem. Later the shadow grows ears and gold eyes that glow inside the black. · **motion:** A separate silhouette rig that by default equals his projected outline, with a 3-frame lag from 7.2 s. At 11.6 s it detaches. The head turns from profile to frontal over 1.4 s (cubic ease-in-out, 4° overshoot), and the brim narrows and splits into two pointed ears over a 20-frame morph. Then a 12° dog-like listening tilt and one 3-frame blink. During the spins it morphs through 5 keyed outlines (96 arc-length-resampled points each): upright human, hunched with ears, arched with forelegs, half-beast, coyote on all fours. Secondary motion: an 8-segment tail chain, ear springFollow, and breathing scale of 1.5% at 0.4 Hz.
- **name:** El nahual entre formas (in-between form, shadow only) · **how_shown:** Seen ONLY as a shadow on the wall during the approach: a hunched upright figure with a coyote head and tall ears, long hanging arms, a stooped man's back and a bushy tail. Its body is never shown. It walks along our side of the street, just outside frame left, toward the bulb. · **motion:** A biped walk on a 1.0 s cycle, heavy and stooped, the head bobbing and turning slightly as it sniffs. Each step makes the shadow larger and higher on the wall (its distance to the bulb drops from 1.9 m to 0.5 m). At 29.4 s the eyes ignite. At 30.5 s it drops to all fours, so its shadow sinks down the wall and off it onto the street.
- **name:** El coyote (animal form) · **how_shown:** A painted 2D side silhouette: lean, tall ears, narrow snout, long legs, bushy low tail. An orange rim on the bulb side, teal on the far side, about 180 contour fur strokes. Its short shadow falls on the cobblestones, because it is a low animal under a high bulb. Eyeshine flares gold-green when it faces us. For the jump scare it is a dedicated head painting in front three-quarter view. · **motion:** It crawls out from under the empty sarape paw first, then the snout, then a full-body shake (0.6 s; spine twist decays, fur strokes whip). Four-beat lateral walk on a 0.9 s cycle with ik2 legs and planted paws, head low with a sniffing bob, ears on springFollow, tail on simChain. It stops, turns its head to us and holds 0.6 s, then slinks left, body lowered 10% with a longer stride. Jump scare: the head bursts in over 4 frames (expo ease-out, 6% overshoot), one jaw snap, the skull bangs the shutter lip.
- **name:** El perro callejero · **how_shown:** A lean, short-haired tan street dog with a darker back, curled asleep at the base of the wall at the right (screen x≈915), rim-lit. Its eyes catch the light and its small shadow lies on the wall just above it. · **motion:** Same quadruped rig with dog proportions. The flank rises slowly while asleep. At 8.4 s it lifts its head and flattens its ears; at 9.6 s it rises halfway with hackles up (back fur strokes lengthen); at 14 s it backs off crouching, tail tucked; at 24.2 s it yelps and bolts out of frame right at a gallop. It is never harmed.
- **name:** El tecolote (omen) · **how_shown:** A small owl silhouette (about 60 px) perched on the power line in the far callejón, next to the papel picado, with two amber eyes. · **motion:** It ruffles once (feathers scale 1.04, then back). At 2.9 s it swivels its head toward us over 20 frames (fast start, slow end). At 16.2 s it drops off the line and flies into the fog: a 0.45 s asymmetric flap cycle, feather chains lagging. Heard once more in the black at 33.6 s.
- **name:** Nosotros (the hidden shopkeeper, POV) · **how_shown:** Never seen. Present only through the camera: breathing, rack focus, leaning to follow the coyote, hands jerking the shutter down in three stuck pulls and slamming it. · **motion:** handheld(t) at 2 px and 0.22 Hz while calm, 1 px while watching the shadow turn, rising to 6 px and 0.55 Hz in panic, 0 during the held breath. Push-ins and pull-backs follow camPath Catmull-Rom curves.

## Sustos

- **t:** 1 · **what:** A subliminal 6-frame swap. On the bulb flicker, the giant shadow of the man leaving our door has a coyote's head (frames 31–36), then it is a sombrero again. · **why_it_works:** It plants the rule of the whole video in the first second (watch the shadow). Paired with «Mírale la sombra.», it reads as 'did I just see that?' and drives rewatches without being a cheap pop.
- **t:** 11.2 · **what:** The whole village goes silent at once: every distant dog stops mid-bark and the crickets click off. · **why_it_works:** Animals sensing what we can't see is a primal warning. The hard cut from a busy night bed to near silence makes the viewer lean in, right before the shadow moves.
- **t:** 13 · **what:** Don Fermín stays perfectly still, not breathing, while his shadow turns its head to us. Gold eyes ignite inside the black shadow, then it tilts its head like a listening dog and blinks. · **why_it_works:** It breaks a physical law we trust without thinking: a shadow must copy its owner. The silence makes the violation unbearable, and the direct look puts the viewer inside the story; it knows we are watching.
- **t:** 20.2 · **what:** The spinning sarape collapses empty on the cobblestones. The pile breathes, a huarache slides out, then a paw. · **why_it_works:** Horror through absence: a transformation with no gore, only a body that is no longer there. The beat of stillness before the paw draws the dread out.
- **t:** 24.8 · **what:** After the dog's yelp, the coyote stops and turns its head to the lens; its eyeshine flares straight at us. · **why_it_works:** A small startle resets the nerves before the climax. The eyeshine is motivated by the light next to our hiding place, so it means 'it knows exactly where you are'.
- **t:** 26.6 · **what:** Its shadow returns walking UPRIGHT, a coyote head on a stooped human body, growing step by step on the wall while the shutter is jerked down. Its huge eyes ignite at 29.4 s. · **why_it_works:** The film has just taught the rule (a growing shadow means it is coming for you), so the viewer reads the danger in the shadow before the text confirms it. The in-between form, neither man nor animal, is the nahual's duality made literal. The narrowing frame tightens the throat.
- **t:** 31.2 · **what:** MAIN JUMP SCARE. After 0.7 s of total silence and a frozen camera, the coyote's snarling head bursts up into the narrowed gap with a death-whistle shriek, a metal bang and a shake. · **why_it_works:** Misdirection: the growing shadow trains the eye on the wall, then the shadow sinks away and the threat comes from below, closest to us. Silence plus the frozen handheld (held breath) gives maximum contrast. The prehispanic death whistle makes the sound culturally specific and genuinely unsettling.

## Música

An original 'prehispanic-rural nocturne' in D minor pentatonic (D–F–G–A–C), fully synthesized in numpy. No borrowed melody, which keeps the traditional «La Llorona» son free for Día 2. Instruments: a contrabass drone on D1–D2 with sul ponticello bow hiss (filtered saw plus noise); a teponaztli (two-tone slit drum, modal wooden bar on D3 and F3), first an ostinato at 52 bpm locked to don Fermín's cane taps, later a heartbeat accelerating from 70 to 140 bpm; a huéhuetl (deep skin drum: sine drop from 90 to 55 Hz plus a noise skin) on the title, on each of the three turns (rising in pitch) and on the number; ayoyotes (seed-pod rattles, granular noise bursts) voicing every move of the shadow; a clay ocarina (sine plus breath noise, slight vibrato) carrying the 4-note nahual motif A–G–F–D, slow under the title, an octave lower after the transformation, and a final long A4 resolving to D on the 7; a low choir pad (formant 'u', cluster D–E♭–F) swelling during the transformation; bowed glass on A5 when the shadow's eyes ignite; a string cluster crescendo under the approach; and the silbato de la muerte (noise through a resonant comb plus a 2–4 kHz FM shriek with pitch bend), used only on the jump scare. The night bed (crickets plus distant dogs) runs from frame 0 and cuts out at 11.2 s. Arc: ambience only in the hook; the music enters at 2.9 s; a hard drop to near silence at 11.2 s; the build from 16 to 20.2 s; silence after the sarape falls; the crescendo from 26 to 30.5 s; 0.7 s of digital silence; the shriek; a long silence with the owl; the resolution on the 7; a soft tail into the standard outro. 2) SHADOW ENGINE. Each caster is a 2D silhouette polygon on a card at depth zc, posed in world meters. For the wall, each vertex is projected from the bulb L(t) onto z = 4.2: S = L + (P − L)·(4.2 − L.z)/(P.z − L.z), then camera-projected. For the ground, the vertices are projected onto y = 0 and clipped to z < 4.2, which produces the correct bend where a shadow climbs from the street onto the wall base. Masks are rasterized at quarter resolution (270×480), blurred with ctx.filter blur(r), where r is the penumbra (bulb radius 0.03 m × (receiver − caster)/(caster − light), in px, clamped to 2–14), then upscaled. Wall and ground color = albedo × (teal ambient 0.10 + bulb irradiance × (1 − mask)), so shadows can only darken lit surfaces and never paint over a character. L(t) = pivot + cable·(sin θ, −cos θ) with θ = 0.02·gust idle + A·e^(−(t − t0)/τ)·cos(2π(t − t0)/1.5): one excitation at 31.3 s (impact, A 22°), another at 34.6 s (stuck jolt, A 18°, τ 1.2), plus 30% of the swing in z, so the shadow breathes in size. Glowing eyes inside shadows are additive glow() sprites drawn after the wall pass, at the shadow-rig's eye anchors. The perforation dots in the black take their brightness from the bulb irradiance at each hole, so they sweep with the swing.

3) SET (all cached once). Wall albedo: a noiseTile cal layer over an adobe brick pattern revealed below a noise threshold, ridged cracks, stains, the terracotta rodapié, the grille window with its own small projected shadows. Cobblestones: rounded cells from hashed points, cached, with specular glints scaled by bulb falloff. 4) DON FERMÍN. Legs are ik2 with foot-lock and heel-toe roll, the gait authored with smoothPath and the limp from asymmetric stance timing. The cane is a rigid stick constrained to the hand. The hand is a paintShape path with knuckle strokes. Walking sarape: simCloth pinned at the shoulders, plus 14 simChain fringe strands, all precomputed.
